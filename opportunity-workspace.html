Why it's built this way

Seven decisions behind this prototype. Everything on screen is sample data.

1 · One stream, many types

Minutes, progress notes, tasks, PoC findings and stage changes are all events on a single opportunity timeline, each typed. Filtering by type gives you the tab view back on demand — the reverse doesn't work.

2 · Due and overdue sit at the top of Pulse

The stream opens with what is late and what lands this week, before any history. Overdue items are the only thing on the page allowed to use full-strength red — that's what makes them register.

3 · Tasks are created inline, from anywhere

Every task has one responsible person — normally the owner, co-owner or coordinator — plus any number of assisting team members picked from the user list. The responsible person answers for the date; assistants do the work and post updates.

4 · Stage gates, not stage guesses

Each stage carries a short exit checklist with a named owner, so progress reporting is a by-product of the work rather than a weekly chase.

5 · Two tiers of access

Owner, co-owner and communications coordinator are opportunity members with full read/write. Technical and development people are work assignees — they see the context and their own items, and post into the same stream without being CRM users.

6 · The PoC is its own record

Baseline, environment, measured results, dated feedback, blockers and a report. Sales quotes from the same numbers the engineer measured.

7 · Calendar and stream are one view

Task due dates and activity share the month grid; clicking a day filters the stream to it. Overdue days are marked on the left edge so a bad week is visible before you read a single entry.
